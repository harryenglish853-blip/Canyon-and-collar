# Canyon & Collar — Visual Style Bible

**Luxury stays. Grounded in nature.**

Everything in this package serves one sentence, delivered in order:

> *My dog would be safe here.* → *My dog would actually love being here.* → *This is not boarding. This is a getaway.*

The first is won with light, space and calm. The second with the dog's own behaviour.
The third with the property itself.

---

## 1. The idea

This is a private desert retreat that happens to be for dogs. It is filmed the way
a boutique hotel is filmed — from the guest's eye level, at the guest's pace.

The camera belongs to the dog, not to the marketing department. Most of the film
sits between 30 and 70cm off the ground.

## 2. Where luxury comes from

| Luxury is | Luxury is not |
|---|---|
| Space around the subject | Gold, marble, gloss |
| Light doing the work | Lighting kit doing the work |
| Honest materials with wear | New, shiny, unmarked |
| Quiet, and the confidence to hold a shot | Cutting every 1.5 seconds |
| One dog, attended to | Twelve dogs, managed |
| Detail nobody was asked to notice | Features listed on screen |

If a frame would still feel expensive with the logo removed, it is right.

## 3. Palette

| Token | Hex | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Sandstone | `#D8C3A5` | Walls, paths, canyon in haze |
| Warm beige | `#E4D5C3` | Bedding, linen, plaster in light |
| Desert clay | `#B4704F` | Canyon rock, terracotta, shade side |
| Sage green | `#8A9A7B` | Planting, grasses, juniper |
| Charcoal | `#33312E` | Staff wardrobe, window frames, bowl rims |
| Deep olive | `#4A4F3C` | Shrub mass, shadowed planting |
| Aged leather brown | `#6B4A32` | Collars, leashes, oak and cedar |
| Cream | `#F3EBDF` | Stoneware, typography, highlights |
| Muted rust | `#9C5B3C` | Blankets, textiles, accent only |
| Soft black | `#1A1816` | True shadow, letterboxing, page ground |
| Sunset amber | `#E0A04A` | Interior glow, low sun, lamp light |

No colour outside this list appears at any size worth noticing. One accent
(rust or amber) per frame, never two.

## 4. Light

The whole film is one day. It has to track like one.

| State | Scenes | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Late afternoon | 02, 06, 07, 08, 17 | Warm and open, long soft shadows, no heat haze cruelty |
| Interior daylight | 04, 05, 09, 10, 11, 12 | One big window, warm bounce off plaster, dust in the beam |
| Golden hour | 01, 13, 18 | Low raking sun, rim-lit fur, natural flare, floating dust |
| Evening | 14 | Deep blue sky, warm windows as the only heat in frame |
| Lamp light | 15 | Pools of low amber, deep shadow, no overhead light |
| Morning | 16 | Clean, clear, cooler shadow side, hard-edged sun bar |
| Macro | 03, 19 | One warm key, black negative fill, everything else gone |

Shadows stay open and warm. Highlights roll off — nothing clips to paper white.
No teal in the shadows, ever.

## 5. Optics

| Lens | Used for | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| 35mm | Property, architecture, landscape, trail | Grounded, honest, present |
| 50mm | Dog with concierge, interactions | How a person sees it |
| 85mm | Portraits, care details, rest | Intimate, isolated, soft-backed |
| 100mm macro | Collar, water, food, textures | The detail nobody had to include |

24fps. Natural motion blur. Subtle grain. Real fur and skin texture.
No sharpening halos, no digital clarity, no motion smear.

## 6. Camera language

Permitted: slow dolly-in, dolly-out, lateral track, dog-eye-level, slow orbit,
macro push, rack focus, foreground wipe, through-door transition, leash match
cut, reflection transition, sunlight match cut.

One handheld shot in the entire film — the reunion (17) — and it is stabilised.

At most one restrained aerial to establish the property, and the film survives
without it. No drone hyperlapse, no whip pans, no speed ramps except the single
slow-motion beat in 07 and the drink in 08.

If a move cannot be motivated by a person walking, it is the wrong move.

## 7. The dogs

Happy is shown by a loose body, not a forced grin. Comfort reads as: a tail
held level and swinging low, a soft mouth, weight settled onto a hip, a dog
that looks away from the camera because something else is more interesting.

Cast a tasteful mix — Golden Retriever, Labrador, Australian Shepherd, Vizsla,
French Bulldog, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel, German Shorthaired Pointer,
good mixed breeds — but never more than three dogs in one frame.

They walk, sniff, stretch, rest, play, drink, look at the landscape, get
handled gently, and sleep. They do not pose.

## 8. The concierge

Sophisticated and approachable. Soft olive or charcoal overshirt, cream or
khaki trousers, dark brown leather belt. No logos. No scrubs unless a scene is
genuinely medical — and none here is.

Staff behaviour is the product. Every interaction is filmed at the dog's level:
kneeling to greet, offering a hand low and open rather than reaching over the
head, waiting for the dog to close the distance. Bodies angle slightly away
rather than square-on. Leashes stay slack.

They are present, not hovering. In the two widest frames they are a calm dark
silhouette well back from the subject, or not in frame at all.

## 9. Hard exclusions

Clinical white veterinary interiors. Kennel runs, cages, wire crates, chain
link. Rows of anything. Chaotic packs. Bright plastic toys. Neon. Artificial
turf. Pet-store signage. Cartoon paw prints. Childish type. Gold marble.
Oversaturated HDR. Heavy vignette. Teal-and-orange grade. Stock-photo smiling.
Anything that looks like it was chosen because it was cheap.

## 10. Typography and negative space

Every frame reserves clean, unobstructed space for type — listed per scene in
`SHOT_LIST.md` and marked on each plate in `assets/guides/`. Nothing crosses
that area: no branches, no fence lines, no bright highlights, no horizon.

Text is composited by the website. Nothing is rendered into an asset, with one
exception: the engraving on the collar tag in scene 19, which is a physical
object and must be struck into metal — crisp walls, oxidised base, a bright
catching edge, letters slightly uneven in depth.

## 11. Audio

Only natural atmosphere: desert breeze, soft footsteps on granite, collar
hardware, dog breathing, water, distant birds, a door, room tone.

Score is minimal and acoustic — soft piano, low strings, organic percussion,
a little acoustic guitar, ambient texture. It arrives late, states itself once
at golden hour (13), and drops out almost entirely for the reunion (17).

Never: corporate ukulele, commercial jingles, cartoon dog sounds, swelling
emotional strings.
